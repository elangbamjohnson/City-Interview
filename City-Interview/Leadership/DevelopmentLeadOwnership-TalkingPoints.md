# Development Lead / Feature Ownership — Interview Talking Points

A rehearsal sheet for behavioral questions probing your ability to lead mid-size features end-to-end and support larger initiatives.

## 1. What This Competency Means Day-to-Day
> **Framing:** "Being a development lead means I am responsible for the success of the feature from initial requirements to post-launch monitoring, acting as the primary driver who unblocks the team and coordinates with product and design, rather than just pulling JIRA tickets."

## 2. Core Themes Interviewers Are Probing For
- **End-to-End Ownership:** Proving you care about the full lifecycle (design, architecture, testing, rollout, analytics) and not just writing the code.
- **Handling Ambiguity:** Driving a feature forward even when requirements are unclear, shifting, or undocumented.
- **Cross-Functional Coordination:** Working smoothly with Product Managers, Designers, Backend Engineers, and QA without having formal authority over them.
- **Technical Tradeoffs:** Making defensible decisions under time pressure (e.g., choosing technical debt to hit a deadline vs pushing back on scope).
- **Unblocking the Team:** Being the go-to person when juniors or peers get stuck, and actively mentoring them through complex tasks.
- **Scaling Yourself:** Delegating pieces of work, reviewing PRs, and supporting massive initiatives where you are a key player but not the sole owner.
- **Proactive Communication:** Signaling risks, delays, or architectural blockers upward *before* they become an emergency.

## 3. Likely Behavioral Questions & How to Answer Them

1. **"Tell me about a mid-size feature you led from start to finish. What was your specific role?"**
   - *Hint:* Use STAR. Clearly separate "what I did" (architecture, delegation, stakeholder syncs) from "what the team did" (specific UI tickets).
2. **"Describe a time when a project you were leading hit a major roadblock or delay. What did you do?"**
   - *Hint:* Focus on your *response* to the crisis. Did you re-negotiate scope with the PM? Did you jump in to unblock a junior dev? Highlight proactive communication.
3. **"How do you handle situations where product requirements are vague or constantly changing?"**
   - *Hint:* Discuss your process for forcing clarity: writing a tech spec, building a quick prototype, or setting a hard "code freeze" cutoff for requirement changes.
4. **"Tell me about a technical tradeoff you had to make to hit a deadline. Would you do it differently now?"**
   - *Hint:* Describe a specific compromise (e.g., skipping a generic abstraction in favor of hardcoding for v1) and how you documented the tech debt.
5. **"How do you keep cross-functional partners (Backend, QA) aligned when you don't manage them?"**
   - *Hint:* Emphasize regular syncs, establishing API contracts early, and building empathy instead of just throwing requirements over the wall.
6. **"Describe a time you had to push back on a Product Manager or Designer because a feature was too complex."**
   - *Hint:* Show that you didn't just say "no." You said "no, but..." by offering a cheaper alternative that achieved 80% of the business goal.
7. **"Tell me about a time you supported a massive effort where you weren't the overall lead. How did you contribute?"**
   - *Hint:* Focus on how you took ownership of a specific slice of the pie, delivered it reliably, and actively made the primary lead's life easier.
8. **"How do you ensure the quality of a feature when delegating work to less experienced engineers?"**
   - *Hint:* Mention pairing sessions, strict PR review standards, establishing clear testing guidelines, and creating a safe environment to ask questions.
9. **"What happens after your feature ships? Tell me about a time you caught a bug in production."**
   - *Hint:* Prove you own the post-launch lifecycle. Talk about monitoring crash rates, analyzing metrics, or pushing a hotfix.

## 4. Red Flags to Avoid in Your Answer
- **Saying "We" too much:** Taking sole credit for a team effort is bad, but hiding behind "we" makes the interviewer wonder what *you* actually did. Say "The team built X, and *I* was responsible for Y."
- **Over-indexing on coding:** If you only talk about Swift syntax and completely ignore the PM/Design coordination, you sound like a pure executor, not a lead.
- **No post-launch reality:** Failing to mention monitoring, analytics, or post-launch bugs makes it sound like you just toss code over the wall to QA.
- **Abstract/Hypothetical answers:** Answering "I usually do X" instead of "On the Checkout project, I did X." Always use concrete examples.

## 5. Quick Self-Check
- [ ] Do I have one concrete mid-size feature example ready, structured in STAR format (Situation, Task, Action, Result)?
- [ ] Can I clearly separate "what I did" from "what the team did"?
- [ ] Do I have a second, different example ready in case of a follow-up question?
- [ ] Can I confidently speak to a time something went wrong, proving I can handle failure gracefully?
